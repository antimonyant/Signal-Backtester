## List of things/terms I learned ##
_____________________________________
1. Daily returns: (price_today - price_yesterday) / price_yesterday
To normalize the gains

2. Distribution of returns often have fat tails or extreme days that push the price up and down

3. Moving Average Crossover: Get the average of a short and long window. Buy once the short reachers over the long window
Relies on the strategy that recent trends will continue but will not work on choppy markets

4. Mean Reversion: Get the z-score to see how many stds the current price is from the mean.
Choose a threshold to enter and exit. Relies on the strategy that prices that deviate too far will snap back.

5. 10x move means your investment is now worth 10x its original amount (corresponds to a 900% increase)

6. Want to compute compound returns. For example, if you made 10% on Monday and 10% on Tuesday, 
you did not make 20% total. Instead you made 21%, because Tuesday's 10% applies to a balance that already grew on Monday
(1 + return1) × (1 + return2) × (1 + return3) × ... × (1 + returnN) = (1 + returns).cumprod()

7. Lookahead bias: If you run your strategy knowing the close price of today, then you are looking in the future to trade which is impossible.
This is why you have to shift the signal forward a day

8. Sharpe ratio: How much return am I getting per unit of risk?
Sharpe = (average daily return / std of daily returns) × sqrt(252)
When you add up independent random variables (the daily returns), the variances add but the stds do not.
You must square root them which is why we have a sqrt(252) term.

9. Std is a measure of risk because a return series that bounces up and down a large amount is unpredictable.
However, a large increase is not really a risk which is a limitation of the Sharpe ratio.

10. Max Drawdown: What's the worst peak-to-trough loss I would have survived?
Keep track of the highest cumulative return up to today and compute how far below that today's value is
